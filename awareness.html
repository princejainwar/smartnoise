HEARING HEALTH · PRACTICAL GUIDANCE

Noise damage can build quietly over time.

Very loud sounds can injure the inner ear in one event. Repeated exposure to loud sound can damage sensory structures over time and may cause permanent hearing loss or tinnitus. Risk depends on both sound level and time exposed.

Use the right contextNIOSH's 85 dBA over 8 hours is an occupational recommended exposure limit. It is not a universal guarantee of safety for every person or every setting.

What research says

How harmful noise can affect people

Health outcomes depend on the type of noise and exposure. Avoid assuming every effect applies to every person.

01

Hearing and tinnitus

Loud noise can damage hearing; tinnitus is ringing, buzzing, or similar sound without an outside source. Noise-induced hearing loss can be permanent.

WHO safe-listening guidance ↗
02

Sleep and wellbeing

Environmental noise is associated with sleep disturbance and annoyance. WHO summaries also describe links with cardiovascular and cognitive health outcomes.

WHO environmental-noise overview ↗
03

Safety and communication

Noise can mask alarms, warnings, and conversation, which may make it harder to notice hazards or coordinate with others.

CDC/NIOSH prevention guide ↗

Practical actions

Small changes can lower exposure

A

Move away

Increase distance from the source and choose quieter routes or rooms when possible.

B

Reduce time

Take quiet breaks and limit time around loud activities. Louder sound generally means less safe exposure time.

C

Protect hearing

Use suitable, correctly fitted hearing protection for unavoidable loud work or events. Follow product instructions.

D

Reduce noise at source

For workplaces and communities, address the source, maintenance, barriers, scheduling, and quieter equipment.

For sudden hearing loss, severe ear pain, or distressing symptoms after a loud event, seek prompt advice from a qualified health professional. This site does not diagnose or replace clinical care.

Make a useful report

Help your community act on recurring noise

Keep a factual log: dates, start/end times, location area, type of source, pattern, and any steps tried. Do not put yourself at risk to collect evidence.

Before you submit

  • Describe what happened without guessing who is responsible.
  • Record time and duration; avoid recording private conversations.
  • Keep copies of reference numbers from official portals.
  • For immediate danger, contact local emergency services.
